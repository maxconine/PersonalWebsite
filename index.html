Ultra Robotics

I had an amazing time interning at Ultra Robotics. I spent the first half of the summer on the manufacturing team, learning how every part of their robots is assembled — soldering connectors and wiring, building PCBs, assembling arms — before moving into my own design projects and putting my own parts into production. It was exactly what I was hoping for: jumping into a fast-paced engineering environment and doing hands-on product development. By day two, I was already making cables we needed to ship robots out the door.

Beyond the hands-on work, it was rewarding to see how a high-performing team operates efficiently — CAD collaboration, task management software, work and assembly documentation, design reviews, clear communication, and fast-paced planning with realistic two-week sprints.

A few things I got to work on:

  • Torso wiring loom redesign — my first project. I designed a custom wiring loom that made assembly easier and added strain relief to the torso wires, cutting assembly time, improving robot reliability, and making field repairs easier.
  • Base structure redesign — took the robot's base from an exposed, messy wiring to a protected, easy-to-assemble enclosure with clean panels and a glowing Ultra logo. Half a dozen design reviews later, I landed on a system I'm really proud of. (Unreleased for now, so no pictures yet — stay tuned!)
  • Manufacturing tools — designed a cable quality checker that detects shorts and miswires in cables and robot arms, cutting troubleshooting time and helping us catch bad cables before assembly. This saved us hours of debugging for each robot with an issue. Also built a custom ammeter logger, homing jig guides for the arms, and assembly guides to help speed up production.

Learn more about Ultra: ultra.tech

System on Chip RISC-V Processor

For my System on Chip course, I designed and implemented a pipelined RISC-V processor in SystemVerilog, optimized for both speed and area efficiency. The processor features a classic five-stage pipeline — Fetch, Decode, Execute, Memory, and Writeback — complete with a hazard unit, data forwarding logic, and branch prediction to keep the pipeline moving. The challenge was a competitive CoreMark benchmark: maximize performance while minimizing silicon area. Balancing those two objectives forced careful decisions at every level of the design, from implementing a 3 stage multiply unit to optimizing branch prediction along with deciding compiler flags and hazard logic. My team achieved the best performance processor in the class of 14 students

Check out the project on GitHub: SOC_Final_Proj

Custom Chopsticks

Every time I had instant ramen in my dorm without chopsticks on hand, I'd tell myself, "You should really make some chopsticks." But finding free time during the school year was tough. This summer, I finally had the chance.

Building the jig itself was fairly straightforward, but tuning it to get my preferred chopstick dimensions took some trial and error. I started with walnut, but the wood turned out too soft for the narrow, rigid chopsticks I wanted — they bent more than I liked. So I switched to lignum vitae, one of the densest commercial woods available. It was a challenging wood to work with since it's so dense but it gave me exactly what I was after: skinny, rigid chopsticks that hold their shape.

I ended up making a few sets for family and friends, too. Now I can finally eat my late-night ramen in peace — with my handmade lignum vitae chopsticks in hand.

Backwards Bike

For a Makerspace grant project, I built a bike that steers backwards — turn right to steer left, turn left to steer right. I cut apart a bike headstock and welded it onto another with a gear in the middle to reverse the steering. I call it the "anti-theft bike," and it lives up to the name: I once watched someone try to steal it and give up in about five seconds. The best part is letting people attempt to ride it and watching the moment they realize it's nothing like it looks. I ride it around campus. Below are some pictures of the bike.

Guitar Making
